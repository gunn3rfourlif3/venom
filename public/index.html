What this facility is

Every figure on this page is counted from the register when the page loads. Nothing is cached, estimated, or carried over from a previous run — so each one can be traced to its source while you are standing in front of it.

National footprint

The electoral geography of the whole country, loaded from the IEC’s own geospatial register. This is the ground the campaign is fought on, and it is complete.

The register checks itself

A missing voting station looks exactly like a complete register until someone goes looking. This panel counts wards with no station, per province, every time it loads — which is how the system found 2,366 stations missing across 354 Gauteng wards while every other province read clean.

What polling day produces

A station does not produce one result slip. A metro station issues two ballots and a local-municipality station three, so the work is a third larger than a station count suggests. A system that got this wrong would report full coverage while losing two counts in three.

Every slip is transcribed twice, by different people, and disagreements are surfaced rather than averaged.

What it does on the night

An independent count. Marshals photograph the result slip posted at their own station; every slip is transcribed twice by different people and disagreements are surfaced rather than averaged. That gives the party its own tally, station by station, built from the same documents the IEC counts from — so a discrepancy can be named and evidenced rather than alleged.

This is what observer missions call a parallel vote tabulation. It is the capability the rest of the system exists to serve.

Where the capability stands

What is running today, what is built and waiting on one input, and what needs a decision upstream. Nothing is listed as working that is not.

What switching it fully on requires

Five things, none of them engineering. Each one is a decision or a credential rather than a build.

    Station readiness

    Every voting station, placed on its real IEC coordinates. Switch to Gaps only to see where we have nobody — which is the map that matters before polling day.

    Register completeness

    Whether the register holds voting stations for every ward. On 22 October it was short 2,366 stations across 354 Gauteng wards and nothing reported it — this panel is here so that cannot happen quietly again.
